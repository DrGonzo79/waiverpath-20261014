## Buyer, pain, and status quo

The initial buyer is the adult child or spouse of a retirement, SSDI, or survivor-benefit recipient who receives an overpayment notice—often for $3,500–$85,000—and is at risk of losing a large share of the monthly check. The caregiver already does the paperwork, but gets inconsistent phone answers, free forms without evidence strategy, locally constrained legal aid, or a private-lawyer quote around $3,000.

The report estimates roughly two million overpayment notices per year and cites $23B in uncollected overpayments at FY2023. Since April 25, 2025, new Title II cases reportedly default to 50% withholding; SSI remains at 10%. The report says recipients often have about 30 days before withholding and roughly 90 days before automatic recovery starts, but production guidance must verify the actual notice and current official rules.

## Wedge, gap, and why now

The proposed wedge is a packaged, flat-fee middle between self-service forms and counsel: first an educational readiness check, then a $299 “Stop the Bleed” filing around SSA-634, a $900 SSA-632-BK waiver package, and $49/month Case Watch. The core gap is not form access; it is turning a notice, household evidence, and fault/hardship facts into a reviewable next-step packet.

The policy change magnifies immediate cash-flow pain while reported SSA staffing fell from about 57,000 toward 50,000. The strongest narrow product is therefore a deadline-and-cash-pressure triage tool plus human-reviewed document checklist. It must stop short of representation until fee-approval, accreditation, and state unauthorized-practice boundaries are resolved.

## Competitor map

- **SSA:** official forms, account upload, phone line, and possible quick waiver for small balances; trusted but limited hand-holding.
- **DisabilitySecrets / Nolo:** incumbent educational content and lawyer directory; lawyers reportedly charge around a $3,000 retainer where they accept matters.
- **State legal-aid organizations:** trusted and sometimes represent claimants, but gated by income and geography with limited capacity.
- **24help.org:** guided form support near the DIY end.
- **JustAnswer:** paid expert Q&A, not done-for-you preparation.
- **Allsup:** scaled non-attorney disability representation and an employer/carrier reimbursement product, not this family-facing wedge.
- **Binder & Binder:** known non-attorney representation brand focused on disability claims rather than overpayment waivers.

The moat is not the questionnaire. A credible moat could become audited outcomes, policy-update operations, partner trust, and a compliant representative network.

## Demand signals and evidence limits

- The report cites about 4,400 U.S. monthly searches for “social security overpayment,” 1,600 for “ssa-632,” and 390 for “social security overpayment attorney” at about $10.31 CPC.
- Reddit and news comments include five-figure debts, historic mistakes, disabled/single-parent households, and adult children doing the work.
- The report cites an estimated $7B additional recovery over ten years from higher withholding.
- The napkin model assumes 50 qualified leads/month, 20% core close rate, ten cases, and $900 blended price, yielding about $9K/month and ~$108K during year-one ramp.

Those conversion, partnership, channel, and family-payment assumptions are unverified. Problem search volume does not prove trust in a non-attorney service or willingness to pay $900.

## GTM and offer ladder

Avoid broad paid search first. Show up in existing high-intent rooms: carefully moderated educational posts, caregiver communities, benefits counselors, elder-law and legal-aid overflow partners, disability advocates, and congressional constituent-services referrals. The CTA to test is a free privacy-safe readiness review followed by an explicit $299 or $900 price-accept question. Do not imply affiliation with SSA.

## Biggest risks

1. Paid form preparation crosses representative-fee or state unauthorized-practice boundaries.
2. Families will seek help but not pay the proposed price.
3. Handling notices, finances, disability, and identity data creates severe security obligations.
4. A wrong deadline or eligibility suggestion causes material harm.
5. Policy reversal from 50% to 10% softens urgency.
6. Distribution through trust networks is slower than the search volume suggests.

## Build prompts from the report
