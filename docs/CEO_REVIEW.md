# CEO review

## Challenge

The report’s market pain is real, but the proposed business leaps from “people are panicked” to “families will pay $900 to a new non-attorney brand.” Search volume is problem demand, not product demand. The most attractive feature—done-for-you filing—is also the largest regulatory and harm surface. A form assembler is copyable, a public consumer funnel faces expensive trust acquisition, and mishandling one deadline or identity document can erase the company.

## Decisions after review

1. **Narrow the ICP** to an adult child handling a parent’s Title II notice with essential-expense exposure; do not target all beneficiaries.
2. **Move the wedge upstream** from representation to educational triage and a human-reviewed evidence map pending specialist counsel.
3. **Keep AI out of legal conclusions.** The demo uses transparent deterministic rules, visible assumptions, and no PII or persistence.
4. **Test payment directly.** Five completions, three explicit $299 accepts, and one compliant paid pilot beat waitlist volume.
5. **Lead with partners.** Legal-aid overflow and benefits counselors can transfer trust; paid search waits until conversion is proven.
6. **Instrument harm, not just growth.** Track policy staleness, human overrides, deadline escalations, deletion receipts, and near-misses.
7. **Kill criteria:** stop if specialist counsel rejects the boundary, no paid signal appears after twenty qualified conversations, or review labor makes the tested price uneconomic.

## Architecture and failure-mode review

Static demo separation is correct because GitHub Pages cannot host Python and real case data should not enter a prototype. Before production, centralize versioned rules, require current-source review, encrypt documents, isolate tenants, record access purpose, and create verified deletion. Never claim outcomes, affiliation with SSA, or live backend deployment. The public app labels all fixtures and assumptions.

## Verdict

**Run a compliance-first concierge experiment; do not launch automated filing.** The opportunity scores 80/100 because pain and CTA testability are exceptional, but regulatory and trust risks dominate execution.
