# Office Hours product diagnostic

## Specific customer and desperate use case

Start with an adult child managing a parent’s Title II overpayment notice while that parent depends on the monthly benefit for housing, food, or medication. The desperate moment is not “learn about Social Security.” It is: “The notice says recovery is starting, phone answers conflict, and losing half the check breaks next month’s budget.”

## Painful status quo

The customer can download forms but does not know whether to challenge the amount, seek a waiver, or request lower withholding; which facts matter; how to evidence hardship; or who will take the case. Legal aid is capacity- and jurisdiction-limited. Private counsel often cannot use normal back-pay contingency economics, producing a gap between free DIY content and a multi-thousand-dollar retainer.

## Narrowest paid wedge

Do **not** begin with full representation. Sell a human-reviewed, fixed-scope “notice triage + evidence map” only after compliance counsel approves the boundary. The public prototype proves the first ten minutes: quantify cash pressure, distinguish routes, and create a checklist. The report’s $299 filing and $900 waiver offer are hypotheses, not validated prices.

## Observation and validation plan

Observe ten caregivers complete triage using redacted notices. Measure comprehension, route changes, missing documents, time-to-next-action, and willingness to pay $299/$900. Ask legal-aid teams to identify where the output helps versus creates risk. Success requires five qualified caregivers to finish, three explicit $299 yes signals, one paid compliant concierge engagement, and counsel approval of the service boundary. A signup is not payment proof.

## Future fit

If the wedge works, grow into policy-versioned workflow, accredited-representative routing, consented document assembly, partner overflow, and outcome benchmarks. The compounding asset is trusted delivery and audited case outcomes—not AI prose or a form wrapper.
